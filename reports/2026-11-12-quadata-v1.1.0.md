# Pipeline quality: larplarpsahur 1.1.0 on Round 3 (quadata)

Evaluation of the results ZIP from the 2026-11-12 run on a Qualcomm Device Cloud SM8850
phone. The figures under "App-reported" come straight from the app's `eval_summary.json`. The
"lenient" and "clean" figures were recomputed from `results.json` with
`tools/rescore_results.py` (section 8). The examples and the spot check come from reading the
outputs by hand.

## Summary

- **The run is complete and clean.** All 560 samples were processed with no errors. It took
  9.0 minutes of wall-clock time for 35.4 minutes of audio.
- **Transcription is good.** Much of the app-reported error is formatting, not recognition:
  "10" against "mười", 她 against 他, and fillers such as "uh". Once that is neutralised and four
  broken references are set aside, CER is **0.5 % for Chinese**, **1.3 %** for Vietnamese (VE),
  **5.9 %** for English (WER 10.1 %) and **8.1 %** for the Vietnamese audio of Vie-Man (VM-vi).
  VM-vi is the weakest track and has real misrecognitions. Overall CER drops from 8.0 % to 5.4 %.
- **Translation is usable, and single-reference metrics understate it.**
  - **Scores:** BLEU is 31–38 and chrF is 55–61. Chinese output scores lower, at 35 chrF,
    because chrF counts characters.
  - **Spot check:** in 48 random samples whose transcript was correct, 41 translations were
    correct or valid paraphrases. 4 had translation errors and 3 were broken by a one-letter
    ASR error.
  - **Accuracy figures:** the app's character "accuracy" (47–58 %) and exact match (0–4 %)
    are not "% of sentences translated correctly". Don't use them as headline numbers.
- **The main real translation problem is pronouns.**
  - In Vietnamese→English, *anh/chị* used as "you" came out as "he/she" in 8 of 16 sentences.
  - In Chinese→Vietnamese, the transcript writes 他 for spoken *tā*, so 22 of 33 sentences
    about a woman were translated with a male pronoun.
- **ASR errors cost about 6–12 BLEU** on the sentences they touch.
- **The dataset has problems.** Four Vie-Man Vietnamese references are truncated to the last
  phrase, one Vietnamese/Chinese pair disagrees, and the E→V references are spoken-style text
  with fillers. These lower the scores without being pipeline errors.

## 3. Transcription quality

### 3.1 Three views of the same output

- **Strict** is the app's profile.
- **Lenient** makes formatting neutral on both sides:
  - digits are spelled out (`6:30` → `sáu giờ ba mươi`, `101` → `một trăm linh một`);
  - Chinese numerals are unified, and 她/他/它 and 的/得/地 are merged, since each group sounds
    the same;
  - English contractions are expanded;

- **Clean** is lenient minus the four truncated references described in section 6.

### 3.2 What the remaining errors are

**VM-zh (Chinese).** 43 of the 54 imperfect transcripts differ from the reference only by 她 vs
他, 得 vs 的, or 7 vs 七. Each pair sounds the same, so these are writing conventions, not
recognition errors. Of the remaining 11:

- 9 are only missing erhua 儿 (哪儿 → 哪, 一点儿 → 一点);
- 1 writes 两年 where the reference has 2年;
- 1 is a real error: the surname 武 was written as 吴 (VM-9841).

Chinese transcription is effectively error-free on this set.

**VE-vi (Vietnamese).** 18 transcripts are imperfect, mostly from a single syllable with a
wrong tone or vowel:

- *thang → thanh* (2×);
- *rác → rét*;
- *chật → chọc*;
- *Hóa đơn … bao lâu → Hạ đơn … bao lô*;
- *Diễn giả → Dĩnh Giá*;
- *bàn thay tã → bạn thay trả*;
- *đang → đen*.

These errors are small for CER but can break the translation. For example, "dùng cầu **thanh**
bộ" became "used **the club**", and "Một phần **đen** ra" became "One part is **black**".

**EV-en (English).**

- **One empty transcript.** EV-2748 is a 6-second clip with no output and language `und`.
  This is the only hard ASR failure in the run.
- **Misheard content words:**
  - *Sarah → Cyrus*;
  - *the storage on my phone → the store Jamba for*;
  - *allergic to peanuts → Vamlosics peanuts*;
  - *Sprint → the French*.
- **Style the lenient profile does not cover.** The ASR writes "5:30 a.m.", "GMT+430" and
  "UTI" where the reference spells out "five thirty in the morning", "greenwich mean time
  plus…" and "urinary tract infection".

**VM-vi (Vietnamese side of Vie-Man).** This is the weakest track, for three reasons:

- **Truncated references.** The four truncated references alone account for 319 of the 1,073
  character edits (30 %).
- **Number format.** 13 references write numbers as digits ("lúc 3h", "150 nhân viên"),
  while the ASR always writes words.
- **Genuinely hard clips.** About a dozen produce garbled output, for example:
  - "Cảm ơn bạn đến đón tôi" → "Cầu ở ban ngày đỏ tối" (VM-1328);
  - "Phía trước tôi là cô giáo của tôi" → "Phì trộn tối là cô cháu tôi" (VM-3775);
  - "Trong trường có bưu điện…" → "Trong trường học, còn một bố điền…" (VM-1506).

  The same sentences in Chinese (VM-zh) are transcribed perfectly, so the problem is these
  Vietnamese recordings. Listen to them before drawing conclusions.
- **Possible content mismatch.** In VM-3528, VM-7215 and VM-7149 the transcript is fluent but
  different from the reference. The audio may contain other words than the reference text;
  this also needs listening.

### 3.3 Language identification

- 558 of 560 language tags are right.
- EV-2748 is the empty transcript (`und`).
- EV-3791 was tagged Vietnamese but transcribed correctly in English; its translation was not
  affected.

The translation step's source-language routing agreed with the track on the same 558. The

## 4. Translation quality

### 4.1 Scores

"Clean" uses the lenient normalisation above and drops the four truncated references. These
matter for VM-zh because their Vietnamese text is the translation reference: dropping them
alone raises VM-zh BLEU from 34.4 to 36.0, and the normalisation adds the rest.

| Track | App BLEU | App chrF | Clean BLEU | Clean chrF |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| VE-vi (vi→en) | 31.77 | 56.17 | 32.03 | 56.71 |
| VM-vi (vi→zh) | 33.74 | 33.98 | 34.66 | 34.86 |
| VM-zh (zh→vi) | 34.39 | 59.41 | 37.55 | 61.06 |
| EV-en (en→vi) | 30.14 | 54.12 | 31.07 | 54.73 |

**Comparing tracks.** chrF counts character n-grams up to 6. Chinese characters are whole
morphemes, so chrF into Chinese is much lower than into Vietnamese or English for the same
quality. Compare tracks with the same target language, or use BLEU, which is tokenised per
character for Chinese.

**Reading "accuracy" and "exact match".** "Accuracy" is 100 × (1 − CER) on the translation:
how many characters an edit would have to change to turn the output into the one reference.
Two correct translations of the same sentence usually differ by 30–50 % of their characters.

For example, "How long has he been using it?" against "How long have you used it?" differs in
pronoun and tense. "非常美味" against "很好吃" is a perfect paraphrase with zero characters in
common. A score of 50 % therefore does not mean half the translations are wrong.

### 4.2 How much ASR errors cost

The table splits each track by whether the transcript was exactly right under the lenient
profile.

| Track | Correct ASR: n, BLEU, chrF | ASR errors: n, BLEU, chrF | BLEU gap |
|---|---|---|---:|
| VE-vi | 82, 34.1, 59.5 | 18, 24.3, 46.3 | 9.8 |
| VM-vi | 106, 40.6, 39.7 | 74, 28.5, 29.6 | 12.1 |
| VM-zh | 166, 37.9, 61.5 | 10, 31.1, 54.6 | 6.9 |
| EV-en | 41, 36.1, 59.3 | 59, 27.9, 51.9 | 8.2 |

Longer sentences are both more likely to get an ASR error and harder to translate, so the gap
is an upper bound on the ASR's effect. An oracle run (section 7) would measure it directly.

### 4.4 Error patterns across all 560 samples

1. **Vietnamese second person read as third person.** In the VE track, 16 sentences use
   *anh/chị* to mean "you", and 8 were translated with he/she:
   - "Anh còn phiếu bảo hành không?" → "Does he still have a warranty?";
   - "Cuối tuần này anh có rảnh không?" → "Is he free this weekend?";
   - "Anh chờ tôi một chút." → "He waited for me for a while."

   These are customer-service dialogues, so "you" is almost always right. This is the most
   frequent pure translation error and comes from the model and prompt in OnSpeak47.
2. **她 translated as a man.** Spoken Chinese *tā* is the same for he and she. The ASR writes
   他, so 22 of the 33 VM-zh sentences whose reference is about a woman came out with
   *anh ấy*. This cannot be fixed from the audio alone. It is not a translation error, but it
   counts against the pipeline.
3. **Word-sense and terminology errors:**
   - *bắp rang* → "fried rice" (popcorn);
   - "giao trước mấy giờ" → "give me a few hours in advance";
   - *bài khóa* → 练习题 (should be 课文);
   - *numb* → *mê man* (should be *tê*);
   - the company "Fossil Gas" → "công ty sản xuất khí hóa thạch".
4. **Named entities.**
   - **Hallucinated country:** in VM-1730, 越南 became "Thái Lan" (Thailand). This is the only
     serious one.
   - **Missing diacritics:** 河内 was written "Hanoi" instead of "Hà Nội" twice.
   - **Kept correctly:** 北京, 中国 and the other 越南 sentences in both directions.
5. **What was not seen:**
   - no empty translation except the empty transcript;
   - no output in the wrong language;
   - no runaway repetition;
   - no truncated output.
6. **Reference style.** The E→V references are literal transcripts of spoken Vietnamese with
   fillers and repeats ("đã trở thành đã trở thành", "ờ"). The pipeline writes fluent
   Vietnamese, for example "Việc sử dụng dễ dàng luôn là một điểm thu hút lớn." That costs
   BLEU/chrF without being wrong.

## 6. Problems in the dataset

| Issue | Samples | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Vietnamese reference truncated to the last phrase. The audio and the Chinese text have the full sentence. | VM-5201 ("Sốt."), VM-0383 ("Được điểm tối đa."), VM-1989 ("Buổi liên hoan khiêu vũ."), VM-5942 ("Rèn luyện thân thể.") | 30 % of VM-vi ASR edits; VM-zh BLEU −1.6 |
| Vietnamese and Chinese text disagree | VM-4999: "gọi vào di động **cô ta**" vs 打**我**的手机 | One wrong reference in each VM direction |
| Audio may not match the text | VM-3528, VM-7215, VM-7149 (listen to confirm) | Inflates VM-vi CER |
| Number conventions differ | VM references use digits ("3h", "150"); EV references spell numbers out; Qwen writes Vietnamese and Chinese numbers as words and English numbers as digits | Strict CER penalises format (fixed in lenient) |
| Spoken-style E→V references | All EV rows: fillers, repeats, no punctuation | Lowers EV BLEU/chrF |
| VM-vi recordings that are hard to recognise | About 12 clips with garbled ASR, while the Chinese recordings of the same sentences are fine | Real ASR difficulty; listen to check recording quality |

The truncation was found by comparing reference lengths. The Vietnamese/Chinese character
ratio is 2.3–3.5 for the middle 80 % of rows and at least 1.6 for every other row; the four
truncated rows are at 0.14–1.15.

## 7. Recommendations

1. **Run once with `mt_mode=both`.** This adds the translation of the reference transcript
   next to the pipeline translation. It separates translation quality from ASR quality and
   settles how much of the 他/她 problem comes from ASR.
2. **Report the dataset defects** in section 6 to the organisers, and exclude those samples
   until fixed. `tools/rescore_results.py` already does this in its "clean" view.
3. **Add a format-neutral ASR score to the app** next to the strict one: number format,
   她/他 and 的/得/地, erhua 儿, English contractions and fillers. The strict numbers make the
   Chinese ASR look 8× worse than it is.
4. **Raise the pronoun issue (4.4.1) with OnSpeak47.** It is systematic, easy to reproduce,
   and affects every Vietnamese→English dialogue. A prompt hint or dialogue context may fix it;
   the benchmark should not patch the model's behaviour locally.
5. **Investigate EV-2748.** It is the only empty transcript; check whether the clip is silent
   or very quiet at the start. Also listen to the VM-vi clips listed in 3.2.
6. **Do a small human evaluation for translation**, for example 30 sentences per track rated
   for adequacy and fluency. Single-reference BLEU/chrF cannot tell a paraphrase from a
   mistake, and the spot check above shows most low-scoring outputs are paraphrases.
7. **Fix a small app scoring issue.** The app's number spelling reads "5:30" without "giờ"
   for Vietnamese (`Numbers.java`); worth aligning with the rule used here.

## 8. Reproduce

```bash
pip install sacrebleu
```

```bash
python tools/rescore_results.py path/to/extracted-results
```

The script prints strict, lenient and clean ASR and MT scores per track, and the split by ASR
correctness. Its "strict" BLEU/chrF must equal `eval_mt_summary.csv` for the non-EV tracks;
if not, the app's metric code has regressed.
